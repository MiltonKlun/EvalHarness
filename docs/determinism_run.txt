## Cross-session observation (2026-07-04) — the other half of the finding

Within the run above (single session), pinning held: all IDENTICAL. But two days later the
weekly live CI run (GitHub Actions run 28697905208) re-recorded fresh answers for the 5
functional cases that completed before the daily quota ran out, and the pristine-replay
regression gate then SKIPPED 3 of them — single_founders, single_kestrel2_thermal,
multihop_thermal_model — because their fresh output no longer matched the committed
recording's cache key. So >=3 of 5 re-sampled cases produced DIFFERENT output across sessions,
with every knob still pinned (temperature=0, top_p/top_k, seed, same prompt, same model).

Caveats: (1) this captures the whole pinned retrieval+generation PIPELINE — it does not
isolate retrieval (embedding order) vs generation text; (2) it was an incidental by-product of
a quota-limited run, not a controlled N-sample study.

Conclusion: within a session pinning held; across sessions it did not. Reproducibility here
comes from the COMMITTED RECORDINGS, not from pinning — which is the whole reason record/replay
exists.

## Protocol decision (2026-08-29): free tier, N=3, one session per day

The study stays on the Gemini FREE TIER by choice, not by constraint. Rationale: the daily cap
(20 requests) forces one session per calendar day, and a day-separated session is a *stronger*
cross-session sample than several sessions crammed into one or two days. The cross-session arm
is the half of this finding that needs evidence, so the constraint pushes in the useful
direction.

Sizing: a run costs 2 modes x 3 questions x N calls. At N=5 that is 30 calls, which CANNOT
complete within a 20/day cap — that is exactly what truncated session 1. At **N=3 the run costs
18 calls** and completes with 2 to spare. So:

  N=3, one complete session per day, >=5 days.

Trade-off, stated honestly: N=3 is weaker than N=5 at *catching* rare within-session variance.
If output varies 30% of the time, three identical samples miss it ~49% of the time (vs ~24% at
N=5). What the design buys back is total samples: 5 sessions x N=3 = 15 samples per
(question, mode), MORE than the 3 x N=5 = 15 originally planned, and spread across 5 distinct
sessions instead of 3. Per-session sensitivity is lower; cross-session coverage is higher.

Command (identical every day — do not vary it, or the sessions stop being comparable):

    make determinism-day
    # or: .venv/Scripts/python.exe -m evals.determinism_experiment --samples 3 --delay 13

--delay 13 keeps the run under the ~5 req/min ceiling; the run takes ~4 minutes.
